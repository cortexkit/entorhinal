## acceptance_sketch
1. A move from A to B, with three fake participants, reaches `released`: B holds the claim at the next epoch, A's participants are released naming B, and both Macs' `agent.where` agree.
2. Each precondition refuses in order, with its named code. A refusal leaves no `agent_move` row and makes no move-record, inbox or door call and no state-changing participant call. Before it, only the allowed reads run: the claim refresh pass (which may update `claim_cache`) and its claim read, `supervisor.list`, `catalog.list`, `agent_data.describe`, `agent_data.busy` and `agent.session_liveness`.
3. These aborts leave every participant unfrozen or unstaged and the claim with its pre-move holder (after a `claimed` abort of an unclaimed agent, the source holds it at epoch 2):
   - from `published`, by either side;
   - from `staged`, by the receiver;
   - from `claimed`, by the receiver before `activating`, with the claim returned first.
4. A diverged participant on B aborts the move with reason `diverged` before anything is imported.
5. `external_not_present` holds B at `picked_up` or `activating` until the fake host data appears. The move then completes.
6. Once B reaches `activating`, nothing aborts the move: a refusal holds, `agent.where` shows it, and the move completes once it clears.
7. A killed handler at every recorded step resumes after restart, and finishes the move once.
8. B takes over after A stays silent past 10 minutes after `activated`; A releases when it returns.
9. A move back from B to A calls `cut.accept` on A and completes. It carries every ref B imported, even when no session was opened on B.
10. `agent.move` admission:
    - Direct prompts with the exact summary.
    - The orchestrator relay needs no prompt.
    - Engram is refused unless lid-close moves are on and the agent is idle.
    - Any other caller is refused.
11. `agent.register_session` on A for the moving agent refuses `agent_moving` until the move ends.

### Vocabulary
- **Source steps:** `preparing`, `exported`, `sealed`, `published`, `releasing`; terminal `released`, `aborted`.
- **Receiver steps:** `picked_up`, `imported`, `staged`, `claimed`, `activating`, `activated`; terminal `done`, `taken_over`, `aborted`.
- A move is **open** on a Mac while its `agent_move` row is at a non-terminal step.
- **The irreversible boundary:** the receiver's `activating` step and the source's `releasing` step. From there on, no path aborts the move. Every refusal holds (table below), and operator abort refuses `invalid_state` naming the step.
- **`reason`** (row and move record) is one of `operator`, `receiver_inbox_full`, `move_too_large`, `participant_refused`, `door_refused`, `cut_author_mismatch`, `diverged`, `claim_moved`, `project_not_checked_out`, `gone`, `receiver_precondition`. `detail` carries the specifics as JSON: `{module_id, op, code}` for a participant, `{op, code}` for the door (a seal that ends `failed` or `aborted` is `door_refused` with `{op: "cut.status", code: <state>}`), the failing precondition's code and its detail for `receiver_precondition`. A row that reads the peer's abort copies the record's `reason`.
- **Move errors:** a row's recorded errors are its pending or refused `cut.accept`, pinned-plan read failures, and claim-settlement mismatches. The health reply's `moveErrors` counts them across rows; `agent.where`'s `moveErrors` lists them for the agent as `[{cut_id, code}]`.
- Every reply that reports a move carries `step`, never a record state alone.

### Where a move runs
- `agent.move` runs on the Mac that holds the agent's claim, or on any Mac when the agent is unclaimed (below). Where the claim is held elsewhere it's refused `not_holder`, naming the holder and its display name.
- `to` is a member author pseudonym or display name, resolved against `member_name` only. A **known member** is an author with a `member_name` row. No repository surface checks whether a pseudonym is currently enrolled: engram only refuses the calling device (`not_member`, `crates/entorhinal-module/src/log_client.rs:20-23`), and `member_name` rows are only upserted, never deleted (`crates/entorhinal-core/src/shared_entry.rs:79-83`). So a member that never set a name can't be a target, and a removed member whose name remains passes; a move to it waits at `published` until the operator aborts it. A pseudonym or name with no row refuses `unknown_member`; a display name matching more than one member, or naming this Mac's persisted self author, refuses `invalid_request`.

### The agent's refs
- An agent's refs on a Mac are its `session_binding` rows (`crates/entorhinal-core/src/lib.rs:142-152`), the only ref-ownership table. A move's plan (which refs, which participant) is persisted on its `agent_move` row.
- **Routing by kind:** a ref is planned for every available participant whose `describe` `ref_kinds` declares its `ref_kind` (`docs/designs/agent-data-v1.md:17,54`), and never for any other. A ref may therefore appear under several participants in the plan and the record; each such participant gets it in every op of the move. Two participants' `export` items with the same `(sessionRef, stream)` are refused by the door `cut_item_duplicate` and abort `door_refused`.
- **One owner per ref:** a `(ref_kind, ref)` is bound to at most one agent. `agent.register_session` for another agent refuses a ref bound elsewhere (`ref_bound_elsewhere`), which also covers moved-in bindings. A ref listed in the plan of an open receiver row for another agent refuses `agent_moving` with that row's `{cut_id, side, step}`.
- **Moved-in bindings:** the receiver's `activated` transaction (receiver step 6) inserts a binding for each incoming ref it doesn't hold, with `registered_by: move` and no host session (`harness` and `session_id` NULL). A ref already bound to the same agent is kept as it is. `agent.session_liveness` answers `gone` for a binding with no host session: no host has opened it on this Mac. A later host start re-registers the ref for the same agent, which part 1 already turns into an update of `harness`, `session_id` and `registered_by` (`.cortexkit/alfonso/specs/ct_00000000-0000-4144-98dd-e6982377aec8.md:206`).

### Preconditions, checked in this order before any move side effect
Until these pass, only reads happen: the claim refresh pass in 4 (which may update `claim_cache`) and its `claim:<agent_id>` read, `supervisor.list` and `catalog.list`, participant `describe` and `busy`, and `agent.session_liveness`. There is no `agent_move` row, no `prepare`, `export`, `abort` or other state-changing participant op, no door call, and no move-record or inbox read or write.
1. Agent authority is cut over here (`authority_not_cut_over`).
2. The identity log is enabled here (`identity_log_disabled`).
3. The agent is live (`unknown_agent`, `gone`).
4. The claim: the handler runs one claim refresh pass through `RefreshQueue` (`crates/entorhinal-module/src/claims.rs:30-49`) and waits for it. The cached claim must then read held here, or unclaimed (`epoch == 0`, not stale); epoch 0 is admitted the same way `check_registration_claim` admits it (`crates/entorhinal-core/src/claim_cache.rs:104-130`). Held elsewhere refuses `not_holder`; a failed pass or a still-stale row refuses `claim_stale`; an integrity mark refuses `claim_integrity_failed`. The source then reads `claim:<agent_id>` with `record.read`; that read's version (0 when absent) is the move's `claim_version`, and a read disagreeing with the cache verdict refuses `claim_stale`. The cache can't supply the version: it stores version 0 for any epoch-0 record (`crates/entorhinal-core/src/claim_cache.rs:135-145,172`).
5. `to` is a known member (above) other than this Mac (`unknown_member`, `invalid_request`).
6. The agent has no open move on this Mac, on either side (`move_in_progress`, naming its `cut_id` and `step`), and fewer than 4 moves of either side are open on this Mac (`move_cap`).
7. Two module sets must be available (as defined above): every enabled module whose catalog entry provides `agent-data/v1`, and every enabled configured module missing from the catalog, which may be a participant that's down. Disabled modules are ignored. A module that lists `agent-data/v1` but whose `agent-data` route open is refused is unavailable. Every ref kind the agent holds must also be in some participant's `agent_data.describe` `ref_kinds`. A failure refuses `participant_unavailable`, naming either the module with its `state`, `not_ready` reason or route refusal, or the kind.
8. Every `session_binding` ref of the agent reads `gone` in `agent.session_liveness`, and every participant's `busy` answers `idle` for its refs. Otherwise refused `agent_busy`, listing each ref with its participant, `reason_code` and `reason`. Liveness answers use `reason_code` `session_active` (for `active`, because an open host can write) or `liveness_unknown`, with the liveness cause as `reason`.
- After a Direct confirmation, checks 4, 6 and 8 run again.
- **Admission commit:** after every check has passed, one fenced-writer transaction first looks up `request_key`: an existing row with an equal `request_body` returns its `{cut_id, step}`, and a different body refuses `request_key_reused`. Otherwise it re-reads the agent's bound ref set and inserts the `agent_move` row at step `preparing`, with its `plan` and `request_body`, only if that set equals the set the checks covered, the agent has no open row, and fewer than 4 rows are open. Otherwise it writes nothing and refuses `agent_busy` ("a session started during the check"), `move_in_progress` or `move_cap`. There is no provisional row and no admission step.
  - `agent.register_session` refuses `agent_moving` inside its own transaction when an open source row exists. SQLite serializes the two transactions: a registration that lands before the insert makes the insert refuse, and one that lands after is refused.
  - A crash before the insert leaves nothing. A crash after it resumes a fully admitted move. A same-key resend before the insert commits finds no row and runs admission again; if both reach the commit, the second returns the first's row.

### The move record
- Subject `move:<cutId>`.
- Body: versioned JSON defined by entorhinal, `{v: 1, cut_id, agent_id, from, to, state, claim_version, participants, reason, detail}`.
  - `from` and `to` are author pseudonyms.
  - `claim_version` is the version of the source's precondition-4 claim read. It never changes after publish.
  - `participants` is `[{module_id, refs: [{ref_kind, ref}], external: [{ref_kind, ref, note, check}]}]` in module-id order, refs sorted by `(ref_kind, ref)`, taken from the `prepare` replies. It is the only source of the receiver's modules and refs: the receiver calls exactly the listed modules with exactly their listed refs, and never calls an unlisted module.
  - `reason` and `detail` are set only when aborted. The record's `detail` is at most 2,048 bytes encoded. A larger one is replaced by `{truncated: true, module_id?, op, code}`, and the row keeps the full detail.
- The source encodes the body right after `prepare`. A body over `BODY_CAP` − 4,096 bytes aborts the move with reason `move_too_large`, naming the limit, before export or seal. The reserve guarantees every aborted body fits.
- The codec refuses an unknown `v`, a `cut_id` that differs from the subject, an unsorted or duplicated participant, a ref unsorted or duplicated within one participant (the same ref under two participants is valid), a `detail` over its cap, and a malformed body, by name.
- Every transition is a `record.cas` from the version the writer just read:

| From | To | Written by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| absent | `published` | source |
| `published` | `staged` | receiver |
| `staged` | `claimed` | receiver, only after its claim write succeeded |
| `claimed` | `activated` | receiver |
| `activated` | `released` | source |
| `published` | `aborted` | either side |
| `staged` | `aborted` | receiver only |
| `claimed` | `aborted` | receiver only, before `activating`, after returning the claim to the source |

- The source never writes `aborted` once the record reads `staged`: the receiver's claim take may already have landed, and only the receiver can return it.
- **write_ids:** each move-record CAS uses the first 16 bytes of blake3 (a workspace dependency, `Cargo.toml:19`) over `move:<cut_id>:<side>:<to_state>`, and each claim CAS over `claim:<cut_id>:take` or `claim:<cut_id>:return`. Bodies are a pure function of the row, so a restarted writer resends a byte-identical attempt. A lost CAS reply is settled the record contract's way: same write_id resent within 64 versions, else read and compare author and decoded body.
- A `version_moved` re-reads, and the writer acts on the state it finds. Races between the two sides settle on the CAS.
- **Authors and the pinned plan:** each side pins the body it published or picked up in `agent_move.plan`. Every later read checks two things. The reply `author` must match the table's writer for the state read: `published` and `released` by `from`, `staged`, `claimed` and `activated` by `to`, `aborted` by either. And `agent_id`, `from`, `to`, `claim_version` and `participants` must equal the pinned plan. A read that fails either check is treated as not read. The side keeps polling and never acts on it (in particular, the source never releases on such an `activated`), and it records the failure as a move error.
- **First pickup is the one exception:** there is no plan yet, so receiver step 1 checks the record against the inbox entry and the cut author instead.

### The claim writes
- Bodies are the full `AgentClaim` (`crates/entorhinal-module/src/claim_codec.rs:14-23`), `reason: null` included. `holder` for this Mac is its persisted self author.
- **Take:**
  - Read `claim:<agent_id>`. With no persisted take body, a version other than `claim_version` aborts with reason `claim_moved` without a CAS.
  - Otherwise, before the CAS, persist on the row the exact take body `{holder: this Mac, epoch: read epoch + 1, cut_id, reason: null}` (`claim_take_body`) and the expected version (`claim_take_version`, equal to `claim_version`). Then CAS from `claim_take_version` to `claim_take_body`.
  - An unknown outcome (lost reply, `version_moved`, or a restart with a persisted take body) is settled only by the record contract's rule: the same write_id resent within 64 versions, else read the claim. The write landed when that version's `author` is this Mac's self author and its decoded body equals `claim_take_body`, the same comparison as `cas_settled` (`crates/entorhinal-module/src/record_client.rs:292-315`). Any other claim state aborts `claim_moved`, with the mismatch recorded in `detail` as a move error.
  - With no persisted take body, nothing was attempted: a claim naming this Mac and this cut is not this move's take.
  - The landed version goes in `claim_landed_version`. `claim_version` keeps the source's observation.
- **Return:** CAS from `claim_landed_version` to `{holder: from, epoch: claim_take_body.epoch + 1, cut_id: null}`, even when `from` held nothing before (an unclaimed agent then ends held by its source at epoch 2). If the CAS loses, re-read; `holder == from` counts as done.
- **Cache after a receiver abort:** before the row ends, the receiver applies its last verified claim read through `cache_agent_claims` (`crates/entorhinal-core/src/claim_cache.rs:22-36,235-249`): the confirmed return, or the `Present`/`Absent` read for an abort before any take. Until such a read succeeds, the row stays open at its step, so `agent_moving` keeps refusing registration.

### The inbox record
- Each member has subject `inbox:<author>`. Its body is `{v: 1, pending: [{cut_id, from, agent_id}]}`, at most 64 entries.
- The source appends an entry right after publishing the move record. Before every retry of the append, the source re-reads the move record. Any state other than `published` means the append is done.
- On every inbox read, the receiver removes these entries without creating a row:
  - entries whose local row is at `activated` or terminal;
  - entries whose record reads `aborted` or `released`;
  - entries whose `to` is not this Mac's persisted self author.
- An entry whose agent already has an open row here, or that arrives while 4 rows are open, stays in the inbox and is retried at the next poll.
- Both edits are idempotent by content: append only if `cut_id` is absent, remove only if present. `version_moved` re-reads and re-applies until it lands. Each attempt carries a fresh random write_id; a lost reply is settled by re-reading.
- **Full:** fullness is seen on the read before the append. The move then takes the source abort path with reason `receiver_inbox_full`.

### The machine-local driver
- **Storage:** store migration 9 adds two machine-local tables, outside the identity journal and never shared:
  - `agent_move`: `cut_id` (primary key), `agent_id`, `side` (source or receiver), `peer` (author), `step`, `plan` (JSON), `request_body` (JSON `{agent_id, to}` as submitted, source only), `record_version`, `claim_version` (nullable), `claim_take_body` (nullable JSON), `claim_take_version` (nullable), `claim_landed_version` (nullable), `seal_sent`, `abort_requested` (nullable JSON reason and detail), `hold` (nullable JSON), `cut_accept` (nullable JSON `{state: pending|accepted|refused, code?}`), `reason`, `detail`, `request_key` (nullable, unique), `attempts`, `activated_at_ms` (nullable), `created_at_ms`, `updated_at_ms`. Index `UNIQUE(agent_id) WHERE step NOT IN ('released','aborted','done','taken_over')`.
  - `move_setting`: `lid_close_moves` (boolean, off by default).
- **`session_binding` rebuild:** part 1 declares `harness` and `session_id` `NOT NULL` and `registered_by IN ('orchestrator','host')` (`crates/entorhinal-core/src/lib.rs:142-152`), which SQLite cannot alter. Migration 9 rebuilds the table to make them nullable and admit `move`, copying every row and re-creating the `session_binding_session` unique index; NULL pairs never collide in it.
- `verify` and `rebuild` ignore both new tables. `agent.move`'s `request_key` lives in `agent_move.request_key`, never in the journal's request-key namespace (`crates/entorhinal-core/src/pending.rs:34-61`). A resend under the same key is compared against `request_body`, so a later rename of the target does not change the outcome.
- **Persistence:** every step is recorded in `agent_move` before the next one starts. At startup every open row resumes from its recorded step. A source row resumed before `published` calls `prepare` again with exactly the persisted plan (idempotent: it returns the first reply, `docs/designs/agent-data-v1.md:25`), never with rediscovered participants. It then calls `export` and, if `seal_sent`, `cut.seal`, both idempotent.
- **One owner per cut:** the driver's task for a row makes every call for that move, one at a time.
  - `agent.move_abort` decides in one fenced-writer transaction against the row's current step. Before the irreversible boundary it sets `abort_requested` and replies accepted. At the boundary step or later it replies `invalid_state` naming the step and writes nothing. On an `aborted` row it returns the row.
  - The driver acts on the request only after the call in flight has settled, with an unknown outcome resolved first. No abort ever runs concurrently with `prepare`.
  - The fenced write that records `activating` applies only while `abort_requested` is unset; otherwise the receiver abort procedure runs. On the source, a request found when the record reads `staged` or later is cleared in the fenced write that records its refusal (`invalid_state` naming the record state) in `detail`, shown by `agent.move_status`; recording `releasing` clears a pending request the same way.
  - Cleanup is restartable. Recovery treats as cleanup only rows with `abort_requested` set at a step before the boundary, and rows already in an abort procedure. Such a row resumes cleanup at startup, repeats every idempotent call, and becomes terminal only after all of them succeed. The source at `published` re-reads the record first, so a request refused for `staged` is never turned into cleanup.
- **Its own task:** the driver runs on its own task, the same way as the claim refresh (`crates/entorhinal-module/src/claims.rs:51-68`).
  - It holds the store slot weakly and takes a store handle only while touching SQLite, never across a call to a participant, engram or the daemon.
  - Machine-local writes go through the fenced writer (`with_conn_fenced`) and never append a journal row.
  - Health stays lock-free. The health reply gains `movesOpen` and `moveErrors` beside `selfAuthorConflicts` (`crates/entorhinal-module/src/main.rs:962-963`).
- **Polling:** while any move is open, the driver re-reads each open move record every 30 s, and once after startup and on engram reconnect. While the identity log is enabled, it reads this Mac's inbox on the same schedule. `cut.status` polls use the backoff below, reset on each state change. Polling cadence uses tokio time; recorded instants (`activated_at_ms`, backoff deadlines) come from the handler's `clock`.
- **Unknown outcomes:** a participant or door call whose outcome is unknown (transport failure, timeout, dropped connection) is resolved with the participant's `status` or `cut.status`, then repeated; idempotency makes the repeat safe.
- **Holds:** while the driver holds on a refusal or a liveness or busy check, `hold` is `{code, module_id?, ref?, reason_code?, note?}`. It is cleared when the hold ends.
- **Refusals, one table** (first matching row wins):

| Code | Action |
| --- | --- |
| any `cut.accept` code | receiver step 6 rule; never a hold |
| any code at receiver `activating` or later, or source `releasing` | retryable codes retry with the backoff; every other code holds with the backoff, shown in `hold`, counted in `moveErrors`; never abort |
| `busy` at `prepare` | retry with the backoff, at most 8 attempts, then abort `participant_refused` with the `busy` detail |
| `cut_not_ready`, `unavailable`, `handoff_not_covered` | retry, doubling backoff capped at 5 minutes, unbounded |
| `external_not_present` at receiver diverged | hold with the same backoff |
| `busy` after the freeze, every other listed code | abort, code in `detail` |
| any unknown code | abort, code in `detail` |

- `attempts` counts retries within the current step and resets on each step change. Tests never sleep in real time.

### Source steps
1. Preconditions pass. Mint the `cutId`. The admission commit records `agent_move` (side source, step `preparing`, the plan: participants in module-id order with their refs, routed by kind, sorted by `(ref_kind, ref)`).
2. `prepare` on every planned participant with its planned refs. Encode the move record body (size check above). Any refusal aborts.
3. `export` on every participant. Record `exported`.
4. Record `seal_sent`, then `cut.seal` with every item, then poll `cut.status` until `published`. `failed` or `aborted` abort with reason `door_refused`. Record `sealed`.
5. Read the receiver's inbox; if full, abort as above. CAS the move record from absent to `published`. Append to the receiver's inbox. Record `published`.
6. Watch the move record.
   - `aborted`: read `claim:<agent_id>` and apply it through `cache_agent_claims` (`crates/entorhinal-core/src/claim_cache.rs:235`) as a present observation; call `cut.abort` and `abort` on every planned participant; the row ends `aborted`.
   - `activated`: record `releasing`. Read `claim:<agent_id>` and apply it the same way; if the read fails, keep polling and do not release. Call `cut.handoff` and poll until `handoff` is `released` or `taken_over`; both mean done. Call `release` on every participant with `holder` set to the receiver. CAS the record to `released`. The row ends `released`.
- **Source abort** (automatic, or operator through `abort_requested`):
  - At `preparing` or `exported`: `abort` every planned participant with its planned refs. If `seal_sent`, resend `cut.seal` with the same items, then `cut.abort`. The row ends `aborted` (reason `operator` for the operator).
  - At `sealed`: read the move record first. If it is absent, abort as above. If it is present, the publish may have landed, so the `published` rule applies to the state found.
  - At `published`: if the record reads `published`, CAS it to `aborted`, then proceed as in step 6. If the record reads `staged` or later (before or on the CAS), the operator's request is cleared and its refusal recorded as above: `invalid_state`, naming the record state and saying the receiver must abort.
  - At `releasing` and after: refuse `invalid_state` naming the step.

### Receiver steps
1. **Pick up:** for each inbox entry with no `agent_move` row, read the move record. Entries removed by the inbox rules are skipped, and so are other non-`published` records.
   - **Identity check:** the record's `author`, its body `from`, and `cut.read`'s `author` must all equal the entry's `from`, and the body's `agent_id` must equal the entry's. On any mismatch, a row is created directly at `aborted` with `cut_author_mismatch`, and the entry is removed. Nothing is written to the move record or to any participant.
   - Otherwise, insert `agent_move` (side receiver, step `picked_up`, `plan` = the body). This is the same atomic slot acquisition as the admission commit.
   - This Mac's preconditions 1 and 2 must hold. In place of precondition 7, checked against the record: every listed `module_id` is registered and ready here, and its `describe` declares every `ref_kind` listed for it. A listed module absent here aborts `receiver_precondition` with detail `participant_unavailable` naming it; one present but not ready holds at `picked_up` with the backoff.
   - **Ref ownership:** a listed ref bound here in `session_binding` to another agent aborts `receiver_precondition` with detail `{code: ref_bound_elsewhere, ref_kind, ref, agent_id}`.
   - For an agent with a `project_id`, the project must have a root registered here (`project_not_checked_out`). Agents without a project skip that check.
   - `unknown_agent` holds at `picked_up` with the backoff (the identity-log replica may lag); `gone` aborts.
   - **Local writers:** `busy` and liveness apply only to listed refs this Mac already has a `session_binding` for, for this agent (a move back). Each must read `gone` in liveness and get `idle` from its participant's `busy`. Refs this Mac doesn't hold skip both and are never sent to `busy`. A failed check holds with the backoff, showing `reason_code`.
   - Any other failure aborts with that reason.
2. **Diverged:** call `diverged` on every listed participant with its listed refs. Any `advanced` or `unknown` aborts with reason `diverged`. `external_not_present` holds.
3. **Import:** rerun the ref-ownership and local-writer checks, then call `import` on every listed participant. A failed local-writer check holds, and diverged runs again before import; an ownership conflict aborts as in step 1. Any refusal aborts. Record `imported`, then CAS the record `published` → `staged` and record `staged`.
4. **Take the claim** (above). Then CAS the record → `claimed`, record `claimed`, and refresh this agent's claim cache entry. If that CAS finds `aborted` after the take landed, return the claim before ending the row `aborted`.
5. **Activate:** rerun the local-writer check, holding at `claimed` if it fails. Record `activating`, then call `activate` on every listed participant. Every refusal follows the boundary row of the table. A restart at `activating` resends `activate` (idempotent) before continuing.
6. **Activated:** after the CAS of the record → `activated`, in one fenced write record `activated` with `activated_at_ms` from the handler clock, and insert the moved-in bindings (above). Then remove the inbox entry and call `cut.accept {cutId}`.
   - On a move back, `cut.accept` lifts the cut this Mac released before; a first-time receiver gets `lifted: []`.
   - `cut.accept` is outside the hold policy, and its outcome never delays the move: step 7's `released` and `taken_over` proceed regardless. `unavailable` is retried in the background with doubling backoff capped at 5 minutes, also after the row is terminal and across restarts, tracked in `cut_accept`. A non-retryable refusal is recorded in `cut_accept` as a diagnostic and ends the attempts. Neither sets `hold`; both are move errors, shown in `agent.move_status`, `agent.moves` and `agent.where`, because a failed accept means this Mac's participants can't write to the door.
   - Participants are told nothing further: `activate` already made the data live, and there is no resume op (`docs/designs/agent-data-v1.md:126`).
7. **Finish:** when the record reads `released`, the row ends `done`. If the handler clock reaches `activated_at_ms` + 10 minutes first (also across a restart), call `cut.takeover` once and poll its status; `taken` ends the row `taken_over`, and `failed` is retried with the backoff. The source still writes `released` when it wakes. This is the only timed action, and it never changes the move record.
- **Receiver abort, one procedure** (also the operator's `agent.move_abort` on the receiver, through `abort_requested`, before `activating`):
  - CAS the record from the state just read to `aborted`, with `reason` and `detail`. From `claimed`, or after a landed take, return the claim first.
  - Call `abort` on every listed participant with its listed refs.
  - Apply the claim observation (above) and remove the inbox entry. The row ends `aborted`.

### Admission for `agent.move`
- `agent.move`, `agent.move_abort` and `agent.set_lid_close_moves` are their own served arms, like `role.take`: each gets a branch in `handle_served_request` (`crates/entorhinal-module/src/main.rs:1005-1027`) ahead of the `write_wait` fallback, and the synchronous path answers `served_path_required` for them as it does for `role.take` and claim refresh (`crates/entorhinal-module/src/main.rs:1124-1132`). They are listed in `MUTATING_METHODS` and `SELF_ADMITTED_METHODS` (`crates/entorhinal-module/src/main.rs:473-520`), so flow-scoped routes refuse `flow_scope_not_admitted` and `authorize_write` passes them to these rules. They never go through `write_wait`, never lock the shared `writer`, and append no journal row.
- From `direct`: through `ConfirmationGuard` and `OperatorConfirmer`, bounded by `OPERATOR_BOUND`, with a summary naming the resolved display name, such as `move agent "ENTO" ("agent_…") to "Studio Mac"`. Refused `operator_presence_unavailable` where there's no presence provider.
- From the module providing `agent-orchestrator/v1`, resolved the same way as `agent.register_session` (cached per route): relaying a move the operator confirmed on the phone. No prompt.
- From `reserved:engram`: only while `lid_close_moves` is on, and only when every precondition holds, idle included. Otherwise `caller_forbidden` or the failing precondition.
- From anyone else: `caller_forbidden`.
- Request: `{agent_id, to, request_key}`. Reply: `{cut_id, step: "preparing"}` once the admission commit has inserted the row; the move continues in the background. A resend with the same `request_key` and `request_body` returns `{cut_id, step}` of the existing row (the admission commit's key lookup, above); a different body under the same key refuses `request_key_reused`.

### Other ops and CLI
- **`agent.move_status {cut_id}`** and **`agent.moves {}`** (open moves first, then the last 32 finished ones): open reads, from `agent_move` only. Each row is returned as its columns, except `plan`, `request_body` and the claim columns, plus `peer_name` from `member_name`. `detail`, `hold` and `cut_accept` are returned as JSON.
- **`agent.move_abort {cut_id, request_key}`:** Direct with confirmation, or the orchestrator. It decides as in "One owner per cut": before the boundary it replies accepted with the row (`abort_requested` set), and the cleanup's outcome shows in `agent.move_status`; at or after the boundary it replies `invalid_state` naming the step. A resend on an already aborted row returns that row.
- **`agent.set_lid_close_moves {enabled, request_key}`:** Direct with confirmation only; setting the current value is a no-op.
- On `agent.move_abort` and `agent.set_lid_close_moves`, `request_key` is required but idempotency is by state, not by a key cache.
- Each of these five ops has an `agent_operation_description` entry (`crates/entorhinal-module/src/main.rs:1943`); the writes are in `MUTATING_METHODS`.
- **`agent.where`** gains `moving: {cut_id, side, step, peer, peer_name, hold?}` while a move is open here, with `hold` omitted when the row has none, and `moveErrors: [{cut_id, code}]` listing the agent's move errors on this Mac (Vocabulary). Each key is omitted, not null, when it has nothing to report, so the pinned part 1 reply bytes are unchanged. A `claimed` claim with `moving.step` `claimed` or `activating` is a move in progress.
- **`agent.register_session`** refuses `agent_moving`, with detail `{cut_id, side, step}`, checked inside its own transaction, while this Mac has a source row for the agent at any open step, or a receiver row at `picked_up` through `activating`: a new session there would write past the cut. `check_registration_claim` cannot cover the receiver, because it admits an epoch-0 claim and an uncached one (`crates/entorhinal-core/src/claim_cache.rs:104-122`). After the move, the source's refreshed cache refuses `held_elsewhere`. The ref-ownership rule above also applies.
- **CLI:** `ck agents move <agent> --to <mac>`, `ck agents moves`, `ck agents move-abort <cut_id>`, `ck agents lid-close-moves on|off`. They go in `crates/entorhinal-module/src/cli.rs`, the hand-written parser (no clap) dispatched from the `ck-agents`/`ckdev-agents` `argv[0]` faces, beside the existing verbs (`create`, `rename`, `retire`, `tag`, `labels`, `list`, `show`, `where`, `repair-workspaces`) in the same style, with their tests there.

### Tests and fences
- **Fakes:**
  - The in-tree fakes enforce the engram rules: door idempotency and author, record CAS, body cap and write_id retention, and inbox CAS.
  - A fake participant implements `agent-data/v1` exactly as the contract pins it: idempotency on `(cutId, op)` with refs compared as a set, a fixed ref set per cut, `abort` refused after `activated`/`released`, and the step machine. It can be scripted to answer `busy` with `unknown` for refs it doesn't hold.
  - Source and receiver fake participants share no state. The receiver learns an external check only from the bytes it reads through the fake door.

## intent
The operator approved AGENT_SYNC draft 11: an agent can move from one of the operator's Macs to another. Entorhinal owns the move. Part 1 (on main) gave entorhinal the claim cache, `agent.where`, the session registry and liveness, member names and role claims. This campaign builds the move itself:

- `agent.move {agent_id, to}` runs on the Mac that holds the agent (or on any Mac while the agent is still unclaimed) and moves it to another enrolled Mac.
- One encrypted compare-and-swap move record per move, in engram's cloud, coordinates the two Macs. Its states are `published`, `staged`, `claimed`, `activated`, then `released`, or `aborted`.
- A durable machine-local driver on each Mac advances its side of the move and survives restarts and sleep. No move-record transition is ever inferred from a timeout. The one timed action is the receiver's door takeover, 10 minutes after `activated`, when the claim has already moved.
- Each participant module moves its own data through the `agent-data/v1` contract (commons crate `cortexkit-role-agent-data`, from the agreed draft `docs/designs/agent-data-v1.md`). The bytes travel through engram's session door.

Fork resolution (the operator choosing a side when both Macs changed an agent) is part 3. Here, a diverged receiver stops the move and reports it.





## non_goals
- Fork resolution: `agent.resolve_fork`, minting a fork agent, the fork choice UI. A diverged receiver aborts the move with reason `diverged`.
- Choosing the target automatically. `to` is always given.
- The lid-close trigger. Engram calls `agent.move`; this campaign only admits that call under the operator's standing setting.
- Phone UI. The orchestrator relay path is admitted; the phone itself is CKIOS's.
- Implementing any participant. Participants (MC, BROCA, OC, PI, THALAMUS) implement `agent-data/v1` in their own repos.
- Changes to engram, the daemon or the commons crate.
- Recovering a move stuck past the irreversible boundary. A non-retryable participant refusal at `activate` or `release` holds the move with the claim on the receiver and the source's participants frozen; `agent.where` shows the hold and operator abort refuses `invalid_state`. A Mac that loses its `agent_move` row after the record reads `staged` strands the move the same way. Manual recovery or a forced path is part 3.
